A Stent Fixes 48 Millimeters; Cholesterol Affects All Arteries

Why heart attack patients suffer recurrent events in other coronary vessels

Mechanical Reality

The Stent is a Local Fix

  • Covers exactly 48 millimeters of his mid-LAD.
  • Re-opens the blocked lumen to stop acute ischemia.
  • Exerts zero biological effect on upstream or downstream coronary arteries!
Local salvage ≠ Systemic cure
Biological Reality

Cholesterol Fuels Recurrent Attacks

  • With an LDL of 173 mg/dL, cholesterol particles continuously invade arterial walls across the heart.
  • Mr. Hong already has a 40-50% stenosis in his proximal LAD!
  • PROSPECT Study: 50% of recurrent heart attacks occur in non-culprit plaques, not the stented site!
Plaque stabilization prevents the next attack!
1. Stone GW, et al. A prospective natural-history study of coronary atherosclerosis (PROSPECT). N Engl J Med. 2011;364(3):226-235.
2. Libby P. The changing nature of atherosclerosis. J Am Coll Cardiol. 2021;78(23):2331-2345.

The Target: What is Mr. Hong's Goal?

2026 ACC/AHA and ESC guidelines mandate strict secondary prevention targets

Guideline Standard

The Post-MI Dual Target

1. ≥ 50% Reduction

Cut baseline LDL cholesterol by at least half.

2. Absolute LDL < 55 mg/dL

Aggressive target to halt and shrink plaques (< 1.4 mmol/L).

Source: 2026 ACC/AHA Dyslipidemia Guideline & 2019/2023 ESC Guidelines

Clinical Evidence

Why Must We Reach < 55 mg/dL?

  • Fewer Heart Attacks: Every 39 mg/dL drop in LDL cuts major vascular events by 22% (CTT Collaboration).
  • Active Plaque Reversal: Coronary ultrasound proves plaque volume only regresses below 60-70 mg/dL.
  • Proven Safety: Trials treating down to < 30 mg/dL showed no safety or cognitive issues.
✓ Lower is better, and earlier is better!
1. Blumenthal RS, et al. 2026 ACC/AHA Guideline on the Management of Dyslipidemia. Circulation. 2026. PMID: 41824552.
2. Mach F, et al. 2019 ESC/EAS Guidelines for the management of dyslipidaemias. Eur Heart J. 2020;41(1):111-188.

The Solution: Combine Statin with Ezetimibe

Targeting both liver production and gut absorption closes the therapeutic gap

Pillar 1: Liver

High-Intensity Statin

  • Action: Inhibits cholesterol production in liver cells.
  • Dose: Atorvastatin 80 mg or Rosuvastatin 20-40 mg.
  • Efficacy: Drops blood LDL by ∼50%.
First-line foundation for all post-MI patients
Pillar 2: Gut

Ezetimibe 10 mg

  • Action: Blocks cholesterol absorption in the intestine.
  • Synergy: Adds another 15% to 20% drop!
  • IMPROVE-IT Trial: In 18,000 post-MI patients, reached LDL 54 mg/dL and prevented heart attacks.
Blocks gut absorption feedback loop
✓ Statin + Ezetimibe delivers ~65% total reduction — reaching < 55 mg/dL! Target Reached
1. Cannon CP, et al. IMPROVE-IT Trial. N Engl J Med. 2015;372(25):2387-2397. PMID: 26039521.
2. Kim BK, et al. RACING Trial. Lancet. 2022;400(10349):380-390. PMID: 35863373.

What If LDL Remains High? Add a PCSK9 Inhibitor

The powerful third line of defense for very-high-risk patients

Pillar 3

How PCSK9 Inhibitors Work

  • The Problem: PCSK9 destroys LDL receptors on liver cells.
  • The Treatment: Injections (Evolocumab / Alirocumab / Inclisiran) block PCSK9.
  • Result: Liver cells keep their receptors and rapidly clear cholesterol from the blood!
  • Power: Adds another 50% to 60% reduction.
Receptors recycle over 150 times!
Trial Evidence

FOURIER & ODYSSEY Trials

  • Massive Drop: Lowered median LDL to 30-40 mg/dL.
  • Event Reduction: 15% fewer heart attacks and strokes.
  • Survival: Significantly reduced overall mortality in high-baseline patients!
Proven mortality reduction in high baseline risk
✓ Triple therapy achieves >80% overall LDL reduction for very-high-risk patients! Maximum Defense
1. Sabatine MS, et al. FOURIER Trial. N Engl J Med. 2017;376:1713-1722. PMID: 28304224.
2. Schwartz GG, et al. ODYSSEY OUTCOMES Trial. N Engl J Med. 2018;379:2097-2107. PMID: 30403574.
